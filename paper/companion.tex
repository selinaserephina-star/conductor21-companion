\begin{abstract}
We report a systematic empirical study of the interplay between the primes and the
zeros of the degree-8 Artin $L$-function $\Lgen$ of the Galois closure of the
Trinks field $x^7-7x+3$ (Galois group $\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$, Artin conductor
$21^{10}$, $\Gamma_{\mathbb C}(s)^4$, $\varepsilon=+1$) --- the central object of
our conductor-21 program \cite{mainpaper}. Working from 153 rigorously certified
zeros ($\gamma\le 27.911$, the first computed for this $L$-function) and exact
Frobenius data for every prime up to $10^{10}$, we measure: (i) the explicit
formula as a numerical identity at correlation $1.000$, including the forced
silence of the ramified prime $7$; (ii) a \emph{blindness suite}: the Frobenius
class carries no information about prime twinning, about any additive gap
$g\le 60$, or about the affine pairs $(p,2p\pm1)$, at the $10^{-3}$ level per
statistic, while the same data reproduce the classical Hardy--Littlewood
singular-series structure to $0.1\%$; (iii) Chebotarev races whose biases
($\mu_C=1-r_2(C)$, i.e.\ $-21$ for the split class) and oscillation shapes are
predicted by our zero tables at correlations $0.81$--$0.97$ over six decades;
(iv) the spectral statistics of the low zeros (GUE-consistent, symmetry type
$\mathrm{SO(even)}$); and (v) the Montgomery pair-correlation form factor of the
\emph{bulk} spectrum, measured from primes via short-interval variance: the high
conductor places every height $\gamma\gtrsim 2$ in the correlation-sensitive
regime $\alpha<1$ already at $x=10^8$, and the measured variance follows the GUE
ramp $F(\alpha)=\alpha$ to $\sim\!1\%$ over $\alpha\approx0.22$--$0.74$, at
heights up to $\gamma\sim10^4$ --- four hundred times beyond the certified
spectrum --- with uncorrelated zeros excluded at $\chi^2\approx5\times10^5$.
All results are consistency measurements against classical predictions
(Montgomery; Goldston--Montgomery; Hardy--Littlewood; Rubinstein--Sarnak;
Katz--Sarnak); no novelty is claimed for the frameworks, and the Riemann
Hypothesis for $\Lgen$ --- the open problem of the main paper --- is untouched.
Every claim is reproducible from the released scripts and data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The main paper of this program \cite{mainpaper} studies a single automorphic
object: the degree-8 Artin $L$-function $\Lgen$ attached to the 8-dimensional
irreducible representation of $\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$ realised by the Galois closure
of the septic $f(x)=x^7-7x+3$ (the Trinks field \cite{trinks}; LMFDB
\cite{lmfdb} Artin object \texttt{8.16679880978201.21t14.b.a}, stem field
\texttt{7.3.4202539929.1}, whose displayed polynomial $x^7-7x-3$ is equivalent
to ours under $x\mapsto-x$). Its analytic data are: conductor
$Q=21^{10}=3^{10}7^{10}$, gamma factor $\Gamma_{\mathbb C}(s)^4$, root number
$\varepsilon=+1$; the character $\chi_8$ is monomial, so $\Lgen$ is also a Hecke
$L$-function of an octic field, and its analytic continuation and functional
equation are unconditional. The main paper's open problem --- the Riemann
Hypothesis for $\Lgen$, equivalently the self-adjointness deficiency question for
the second operator constructed there --- is \emph{not} addressed here.

This companion does something deliberately more modest and, we hope, more
legible: it measures, at scale and with controls, how this object's primes and
zeros talk to each other, and it documents that everything classical behaves ---
in several regimes never before measured for any $\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$ extension.
Three features distinguish the study.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exactness of the arithmetic side.} We computed the exact Frobenius
class (not merely $a_p$) of every prime $p\le 10^9$, and exact $a_p$ for every
prime to $10^{10}$ ($455{,}052{,}511$ primes), with two independent engines
(polynomial factorization over $\mathbb F_p$ via FLINT; an overflow-safe modular
Frobenius kernel) that agree to $10^{-8}$ on overlapping data.
\item \textbf{First zero tables.} The 153 certified zeros ($\gamma\le27.911$;
eight exact, the rest ball-arithmetic brackets) and the low-zero tables of the
companion characters $\chi_3\bar\chi_3$, $\chi_6$, $\chi_7$ of the same Galois
group are, to our knowledge, the first computed for these $L$-functions.
\item \textbf{The conductor as an instrument.} $Q=21^{10}$ makes the zeros so
dense ($2\pi\bar\rho(\gamma)=\ln Q-8\ln2\pi+8\ln\gamma\approx15.7+8\ln\gamma$)
that pair-correlation information is accessible from primes at heights and
scales where $\zeta$ shows only diagonal behaviour (\S\ref{sec:ramp}).
\end{enumerate}

Throughout, predictions conditional on standard hypotheses (GRH for the family,
Linear Independence, cluster expansions) are labelled as such; measurements are
unconditional exact counting. Where a fluctuation appeared during the campaign
(three did), it was either explained by an exact prediction or retired by
independent data; we report these episodes as part of the record
(\S\ref{sec:flags}).

\section{Data and instruments}\label{sec:data}
\paragraph{Zeros.} The certified table: $\gamma_1=0.98638643\ldots$ through
$\gamma_{153}=27.911$, produced by the program's ball-arithmetic FFT engine and
certified by a Turing-style count \cite{mainpaper}. Companion tables (numerically
anchored, PARI): $\chi_3$-pair 15 zeros ($\gamma\le5.31$), $\chi_6$ 12
($\le4.85$), $\chi_7$ 10 ($\le4.14$).

\paragraph{Counting function.} With
$\theta(T)=\tfrac{T}{2}\ln Q-4T\ln2\pi+4\,\mathrm{arg}\,\Gamma(\tfrac12+iT)$ and
$\bar N(T)=\theta(T)/\pi+1$, the certified staircase is reproduced with no free
parameters: slope $0.99895$, mean unrenormalised spacing $0.9963$, fluctuation
$S(T)$ of mean $0$ and standard deviation $0.365$. This cross-validates the zero
list against the conductor/gamma data.

\paragraph{Primes.} Exact Frobenius classes for all $p\le10^9$
(FLINT factorization of $f$ mod $p$, $22\,\mu$s/prime, cached); exact $a_p$ for
$p\in(10^9,10^{10}]$ ($404{,}204{,}977$ primes, overflow-safe batch kernel,
$47$ minutes). Chebotarev equidistribution holds to five digits at $10^{10}$:
value fractions $(8,0,-1,1)$ at $(0.00595,\,0.37500,\,0.33331,\,0.28574)$ against
$(1/168,\,3/8,\,1/3,\,2/7)$. The two engines agree on overlapping increments at
correlation $1.000000$ (rms ratio $1.00000001$).

\section{The explicit formula as an identity (instrument calibration)}
\label{sec:identity}
For a window $w$ on $[0,\gamma_{\max}]$ define the resonance
$F(x)=\sum_j w(\gamma_j)\cos(x\gamma_j)$ and subtract the smooth main term
$\int w(\gamma)\cos(x\gamma)\bar\rho(\gamma)\,d\gamma$. The explicit formula
predicts the residual as
$-\tfrac1{2\pi}\sum_n \Lambda_\chi(n)n^{-1/2}\,[\hat w(x-\ln n)+\hat w(x+\ln n)]$
with $\Lambda_\chi(p^k)=\bigl(\sum_i\alpha_i^k\bigr)\ln p$ computed from the
exact local factors of each class. Measured against predicted:
correlation $+1.000$ for $\Lgen$ (residual rms $0.904$ = predicted rms $0.904$)
and $+1.000$ for the $\zeta$ control (600 zeros) through the identical pipeline
(Fig.~\ref{fig:res}).

This is an identity check, not a discovery --- but a stringent end-to-end one:
zero list, analytic conductor, and class local factors certify one another
through an independent computation. Fingerprints land exactly: the genome is
\emph{silent at $\ln 7$} (ramified, trivial local factor: measured $+0.438$
against a predicted neighbour-tail of $+0.436$, while $\zeta$ sings there at
$-54.0$); class 2A is silent at $\ln p$ but loud at $\ln p^2$
($\sum\alpha_i^2=8$); class 4A is silent at both.

\section{Spectral statistics of the low zeros}\label{sec:lowzeros}
Unfolding by the exact $\theta/\pi$ (no fit), the 153 certified zeros give
spacing variance $\mathrm{Var}(s)=0.145$, inside the finite-$N$ GUE band
$[0.140,0.219]$ (median $0.176$; $z=-1.53$, one-sided $p\approx0.06$) and more
than $10\sigma$ from Poisson; nearest-neighbour repulsion $P(s<\tfrac12)=0.079$;
number variance $\Sigma^2(L)$ $10$--$15\%$ below GUE for $L\le8$ (a
non-significant finite-$N$ rigidity). The polynomial and exact unfolds agree to
the digit, so the mild over-rigidity is a property of the sample, not the
unfolding.

The symmetry type is fixed by representation theory: Frobenius--Schur indicator
$+1$ and $\varepsilon=+1$ give \textbf{orthogonal $\mathrm{SO(even)}$}, with
low-lying one-level density $W(x)=1+\frac{\sin2\pi x}{2\pi x}$. The first scaled
ordinate $x_1=\theta(\gamma_1)/\pi=1.256$ sits on the attracted side (SO(even)
mean $1.71$, unitary $2.03$, symplectic $2.34$), consistent with but not probative
of the type: bulk two-level statistics are type-independent (Katz--Sarnak
universality), and a single $L$-function cannot resolve the family density. The
family average remains the real test.

\section{What the genome does not know: the blindness suite}\label{sec:blind}
The Hardy--Littlewood singular series \cite{hl} --- the arithmetic of prime
pairs --- is built entirely from congruence obstructions, i.e.\ from
\emph{abelian} data.
$\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$ is simple: the extension has no abelian quotient, hence no
congruence channel, and the heuristic prediction is that Frobenius carries no
pair information whatsoever. We measured this in three geometries.

\paragraph{Twins.} Over the $440{,}310$ twin pairs $(p,p+2)$ with $7<p\le10^8$:
$\langle a_pa_{p+2}\rangle=-0.0020\pm0.0015$ ($z=-1.3$); joint $5\times5$ class
table $\chi^2=11.1$ on 16 dof ($p\approx0.80$). The same primes give
$\pi_2(10^8)=440{,}312$ against the Hardy--Littlewood prediction $440{,}473$ ---
ratio $0.9996$: twinning is loudly present in the data; the genome sees none of
it.

\paragraph{All gaps $g\le60$, to $10^9$.} Thirty even gaps, three windows
($10^8$; $10^9$; the disjoint decade), $3.0$--$6.9$ million pairs per gap at
$10^9$. No gap crosses the 30-test Bonferroni threshold in any window
(max $|\mathrm{corr}| = 0.00101$; per-gap s.e.\ $0.0003$--$0.0005$); the $z$-ensembles are
unit-variance noise (std $1.13/0.95/0.96$); the class-level $\chi^2$ totals sit
on their dof ($475/480$, $480/480$, $462/480$). Meanwhile the same pair counts
reproduce the singular-series sawtooth
$\prod_{p\mid g,\,p>2}\frac{p-1}{p-2}$ (range $1.00$--$2.67$) point by point:
mean measured/HL ratio $0.9989$, worst deviation $0.0015$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:gapmap}).

\paragraph{Affine pairs.} Sophie-Germain pairs $(p,2p+1)$ ($N=1{,}775{,}672$,
$p\le5\times10^8$): $\langle a_pa_q\rangle=-0.0002\pm0.0008$ ($z=-0.28$); count
against the corresponding $2C_2\int dx/(\ln x\ln 2x)$ prediction: ratio
$0.9999$. Likewise $(p,2p-1)$: $z=-0.46$.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figs/fig_gapmap.png}
\caption{The gap-blindness map at $10^8$ and $10^9$. Top: genome Frobenius
correlation $\langle a_pa_{p+g}\rangle$, flat at zero for all even $g\le60$.
Bottom: the same pairs resolve the classical Hardy--Littlewood singular-series
sawtooth to three digits.}\label{fig:gapmap}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Verdict: uniform blindness at the $10^{-3}$ level per statistic},
measured against a loudly structured classical background --- to our knowledge
the first such measurement for a $\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$ extension. One corollary
deserves emphasis, since it answers a question we are often asked: the
twin-prime constant is an abelian phenomenon, and this maximally non-abelian
channel measurably carries none of it. The conductor-21 program is structurally
orthogonal to the twin prime conjecture; no route is claimed or implied.

\section{Chebotarev races predicted by our own zeros}\label{sec:races}
Let $E'_C(x)=\frac{\ln x}{\sqrt x}\bigl[\frac{|G|}{|C|}\pi_C(x)-
\pi_{\mathrm{unram}}(x)\bigr]$ for the observable classes
$C\in\{1A,2A,4A,3A,7\}$ (the septic's cycle types merge $7A\cup7B$, which
conveniently makes all class-averaged character weights real). Following
Rubinstein--Sarnak \cite{rs} and Ng's non-abelian treatment \cite{ng}:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Bias means (exact character theory; no central zeros, as the
tables support):} $\mu_C=1-r_2(C)$ with $r_2=1+\chi_6+\chi_7+\chi_8$, giving
$(-21,\,-1,\,+1,\,0,\,0)$. The fully split primes trail enormously (the identity
has $22$ square roots in $G$); 4A --- the unique class nothing squares into,
$G$ having no elements of order 8 --- is the only class racing ahead.
\item \textbf{Measured to $10^9$} (log-averaged over $[10^4,10^9]$):
$(-32.9,\,-1.8,\,+1.5,\,-0.0,\,+0.2)$, each within its conditional (GRH+LI)
Monte-Carlo spread $(\pm34,\pm7,\pm4,\pm4,\pm4)$; the 1A deficit is visible in
raw counts ($-71$ primes at $10^8$, consistent with
$-21\sqrt x/(168\ln x)\approx-68$).
\item \textbf{Shape from the zero tables:} reconstructing the oscillation from
the four low-zero tables alone reproduces the measured races at correlations
$0.81/0.92/0.95/0.94/0.97$ over six decades (Fig.~\ref{fig:races}) --- the first
dynamical validation of the companion tables, and a direct exhibition of
Rubinstein--Sarnak low-zero dominance (10--15 zeros per character suffice).
\item \textbf{Bias densities (conditional, first computed for a
$\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$ field):} $P(E'_{1A}<0)=0.727$; pairwise lead probabilities
$\delta(1A\!>\!X)\approx0.27$--$0.29$; $\delta(4A\!>\!3A)=0.56$;
$3A$ vs $7$ dead even. Note the structural contrast with the classical mod-4
race ($\delta\approx0.996$): the identity class couples to every character with
weight $\deg\chi$, so its oscillation variance carries the full column norm
$|G|-1=167$ --- \emph{in a strongly non-abelian race the split-prime bias is
large in mean but weak in probability}.
\end{itemize}

\end{figure}

\section{The bulk form factor, measured from primes}\label{sec:ramp}
The variance of $\psi_\chi$ in short intervals, computed from primes, probes the
pair correlation of the zeros (Goldston--Montgomery \cite{gm};
Montgomery--Soundararajan \cite{ms}; for the correlation--prime dictionary see
also Bogomolny--Keating \cite{bk}). Under the stationary cluster expansion
(\emph{conditional}: zeros on the critical line plus the cluster/stationarity
hypotheses --- the measurements below are unconditional),
\[
\mathrm{Var}\Bigl[\frac{\psi_\chi(xe^\delta)-\psi_\chi(x)}{\sqrt x}\Bigr]
\;\approx\;\int_0^\infty \bar\rho(\gamma)\,2|\varphi_\gamma(\delta)|^2\,
F\!\bigl(\alpha(\gamma,x)\bigr)\,d\gamma,\qquad
\alpha=\frac{\ln x}{2\pi\bar\rho(\gamma)},
\]
with $|\varphi_\gamma|^2=(e^\delta-2e^{\delta/2}\cos\gamma\delta+1)/(\tfrac14+\gamma^2)$
and $F$ the Montgomery form factor \cite{montgomery}: $F=\min(\alpha,1)$ for
GUE, $F\equiv1$ for
uncorrelated zeros. The decisive regime is $\alpha<1$. For $\Lgen$,
$2\pi\bar\rho=15.7+8\ln\gamma$, so \textbf{at $x=10^8$ every height
$\gamma\gtrsim2$ is correlation-sensitive}, and GUE vs.\ uncorrelated differ by a
factor $2$--$5$; for $\zeta$ at the same $(x,\delta)$, $\alpha\approx3$--$4$ and
the prediction is diagonal --- the built-in control. In the deep regime the GUE
ramp makes the density cancel exactly, giving the parameter-free identity
$\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{GUE}}=\delta\ln x$ (primes behaving as uncorrelated signs).

Measured over 26 interval lengths $\delta\in[10^{-4},0.3]$ and windows
$[10^7,10^8]$, $[10^8,10^9]$ (exact primes), $[10^9,10^{11}]$ (the program's
earlier deep-sweep signal):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
window & $\chi^2$ vs GUE & $\chi^2$ vs uncorrelated & mean $R=\mathrm{Var}/\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{unc}}$ \\
\midrule
$[10^7,10^8]$, $\delta\le0.05$ (20 pts) & $5.5$ & $585{,}788$ & $0.327$ (GUE: $0.316$)\\
$[10^8,10^9]$, $\delta\le0.05$ (20 pts) & $11.7$ & $470{,}710$ & meas/GUE $=1.009$ at $\delta\le0.01$\\
$\zeta$ control (both windows) & \multicolumn{3}{c}{measured/predicted $=0.996$, $0.994$}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The three windows ride three separate GUE curves --- the $\ln x$-dependence is
itself a confirmed prediction (Fig.~\ref{fig:ramp}) --- and the deep-regime
identity lands at bench/GUE $=1.001$. Effective coverage:
$\alpha\approx0.22$--$0.74$ at heights up to $\gamma\sim10^4$, four hundred times
beyond the certified spectrum. \textbf{This is the first two-level measurement of
this spectrum in the bulk, and it is GUE at the percent level} --- consistency
with the expected universality class \cite{ks}, not discovery, and not evidence
concerning RH. (At $\delta\gtrsim0.15$ the statistic is dominated by the few
lowest actual zeros --- one realization, under half an oscillation per window ---
and both the ensemble prediction and the error estimate degrade; that regime is
excluded by design.)

Two extensions complete the picture. \emph{Gaussianity:} the skewness and excess
kurtosis of the increments match the \emph{exact} shot-noise (diagonal)
predictions $\sum\Lambda_\chi^3/x^{3/2}$, $\sum\Lambda_\chi^4/x^2$
($\langle a^3\rangle=3$): a naive $3.7\sigma$ skewness excess collapses to
$0.85\sigma$ once the shot term is included --- third- and fourth-order zero
correlations bounded at the Gaussian level. \emph{Scale purity:} the ratio
$\mathrm{Var}/\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{GUE}}$ at fixed $\delta$ is flat across five
exact decades $10^6\to10^{10}$ (weighted slopes $+0.022\pm0.015$ and
$+0.005\pm0.018$ per unit $\ln x$); an off-critical-line contribution of
exponent $\beta$ would grow like $x^{2\beta-1}$, so this is a prime-only
consistency check in the spirit of the program's earlier prime-side sweeps ---
corroboration, not proof.

\section{Flags raised and retired (the error record)}\label{sec:flags}
Three fluctuations appeared during the campaign; all three are part of the
result. (i) A $+2.76\sigma$ correlation at gap 4 (one of $\sim$30 tests)
\emph{flipped sign} to $-2.26\sigma$ on the pre-registered disjoint decade
$(10^8,10^9]$: retired. (ii) A $3.7\sigma$ skewness excess was the exact,
predictable prime shot noise: retired by prediction. (iii) A $+2.3\sigma$ drift
in the purity ratio traced entirely to the coarsest corner of the legacy binned
signal; the exact sweep to $10^{10}$ (404M primes) shows a flat ratio: retired
by better data. We consider this discipline --- pre-registered retests, exact
null predictions before interpretation --- as much a part of the companion's
content as the confirmations.

\section{What this does and does not show}
\textbf{Does:} the object of \cite{mainpaper} behaves, measurably and at scale,
exactly as a healthy primitive $L$-function should: explicit formula exact;
low zeros GUE-consistent with symmetry type SO(even); bulk pair correlation on
the Montgomery ramp at the percent level; Chebotarev races carrying precisely
the biases character theory dictates; and a Frobenius channel provably-in-data
blind to all pair structure of the primes, as the simplicity of
$\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)$ suggests it must be. The high conductor, a computational
burden elsewhere in the program, here acts as a microscope on the form factor.

\textbf{Does not:} nothing above bears on the Riemann Hypothesis for $\Lgen$ or
on the operator-theoretic open problem of the main paper; GUE consistency is the
expected behaviour of the universality class, and the conditional statements
(race densities, cluster-expansion predictions) are labelled. Nothing here
advances the twin prime conjecture; \S\ref{sec:blind} shows the opposite ---
a measured decoupling.
